Safety comes first.

Build a safety-first mindset.

Risks differ by route and season. Preparation should be specific to the activity and conditions.

Before departure

  • Research route difficulty, access rules and seasonal conditions.
  • Check current weather and local advisories.
  • Share your itinerary and expected return time with a trusted contact.
  • Choose a group and guide appropriate for your experience.
  • Build fitness gradually and learn about altitude risks.
  • Confirm transport, permits and emergency communication options.

Equipment checklist

  • Weather-appropriate layers and suitable footwear
  • Water, food and emergency supplies
  • Navigation tools and charged phone
  • Headlamp, first-aid kit and sun protection
  • Emergency shelter where appropriate
  • Technical equipment only when required and with proper training

Common mountain hazards

Weather changes, rockfall, avalanches, crevasses, altitude illness, slips, dehydration and poor visibility may create serious risks. Conditions vary by route and season.

Make conservative decisions. If conditions exceed the team's skills or equipment, turn back or choose a safer alternative.

Respect the outdoors

  • Pack out rubbish and manage waste responsibly.
  • Respect wildlife, local communities and cultural sites.
  • Use established routes and camps where possible.
  • Follow permits, guide instructions and local restrictions.
  • Avoid damaging plants or disturbing animals.

A simple pre-trip process

01

Research

Read current route notes and local advisories. Understand the route grade, likely conditions and exit options.

02

Review the team

Be honest about fitness and experience. Ensure the plan is suitable for the least experienced participant.

03

Agree on a plan

Discuss timing, communication, regrouping points and what to do if someone is tired or conditions change.

04

Reassess on the day

Check updated forecasts and conditions. Be prepared to delay, change the route or cancel.

Safety FAQs

Should a beginner attempt a technical summit alone?

No. Beginners should start with suitable routes and seek qualified guidance. Technical mountain terrain demands training, appropriate equipment and experienced support.

Is a phone enough for navigation?

Do not rely on a single device. Batteries can run down and reception may be absent. Learn backup navigation skills and carry suitable tools for the route.

When should a group turn back?

Whenever weather, terrain, time, health or team capability makes continuing unsafe. A summit is optional; returning safely is the priority.

Before you leave

Mountain safety checklist

Conditions can change quickly. This overview is educational and does not replace qualified instruction, local advice or emergency services.

Weather and route

Check the forecast from reliable sources, learn about local hazards and set a turnaround time before setting out.

People and equipment

Match the route to the least experienced participant, inspect essential gear and carry suitable food, water, layers and navigation.

Emergency planning

Share the itinerary, know local emergency contacts, understand coverage limitations and agree on what to do if separated.

On the trail

Recognise when to change the plan

  • Turn back if weather, visibility or route conditions deteriorate.
  • Do not let a timetable or summit goal pressure the group into unsafe decisions.
  • Tell the group early if someone feels unwell, exhausted or uncomfortable.
  • Stay together and follow local emergency guidance when a serious issue occurs.
  • For technical terrain, use qualified guides and formal training rather than relying on a website.
