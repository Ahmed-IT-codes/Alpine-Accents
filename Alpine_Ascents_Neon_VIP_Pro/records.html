CLIMBING HISTORY

First ascents & new routes

Milestones include first ascents, new routes, winter ascents and advances in expedition practice.

What makes a mountaineering milestone?

Records can refer to first ascents, route difficulty, seasonal achievements, speed, or developments in expedition methods. Each type of record requires careful definitions and reliable documentation.

Why records matter

Records help preserve exploration history and document route development. They should be considered alongside local knowledge, teamwork, environmental impact and the safety decisions behind an expedition.

Read the numbers carefully

How mountain records are measured

Record claims depend on definitions, reliable evidence and the organisation that verifies them.

Elevation

Mountain height is measured relative to sea level, but surveys and accepted measurements can be updated as methods improve.

Route and style

Climbing records may distinguish route, season, support, equipment, ascent style and whether a claim has been independently verified.

Human achievement

Preparation, teamwork, judgement and respect for the mountain matter just as much as speed or altitude.

Responsible perspective

Learn from achievements without copying risks

Records are not a beginner checklist.

Extreme climbs involve specialised preparation and significant hazards. Use records to learn about geography and history—not as a reason to attempt terrain beyond your experience.
